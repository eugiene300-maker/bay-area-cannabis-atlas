*Student discounts may exist as retailer promotions—age 21 adult-use ID rules do not bend for campus proximity.*

San Jose State University sits downtown, blocks from plazas and transit, in a city with scarce cannabis storefronts. Students hear campus lore about discounts, delivery hacks, and “everyone has a plug.” Licensed retail reality is blunter: adult-use sales require age 21 and older, medicinal sales with a recommendation can begin at 18, and SJPD registration still decides which businesses are lawful.

ID rules do not flex for campus proximity. A valid government ID showing you are 21 is required for adult-use purchases. Fake IDs are a crime, not a rite of passage. Retailers who refuse questionable identification are protecting licenses. Students who are 18–20 with a physician’s recommendation may shop medicinal lanes where offered; that is a paperwork path, not a party workaround.

Discount myths deserve daylight. Some licensed retailers run student promotions with school ID plus meeting age rules; others do not. A flyer on Instagram is not a guarantee. Ask the registered shop directly. No legitimate discount erases sales tax or Measure U-driven pricing. No coupon makes an unregistered seller lawful. “Student deal” language from accounts that will not name a licensed business is a red flag.

Campus and housing rules stack on top of state law. Residence halls and private apartments often ban smoking and may ban cannabis entirely. SJSU students should read housing contracts. Public consumption on campus grounds and downtown sidewalks remains unlawful under Health and Safety Code section 11362.3’s public-place restrictions. Studying high in the library is not a legal strategy.

Financial and academic caution: cannabis spending competes with rent in Silicon Valley. Impaired driving after a purchase is illegal. Federal financial aid questions have evolved over time, but students with concerns should check current federal student-aid guidance rather than dorm rumor. International students face immigration stakes that make unlicensed product and campus discipline especially unwise—seek qualified immigration counsel for personal situations.

Practical student checklist:
1. Confirm age eligibility before you go.
2. Use SJPD’s registered list—not a classmate’s unmarked delivery account.
3. Start with low-dose products if you are new; edibles wait longer than you think.
4. Budget for tax; legal totals surprise first-timers.
5. Keep product sealed on VTA or in rideshares; consume only where private property rules allow.

Community-college students across the South Bay face the same age gates and often longer trips to San Jose’s registered shops because local storefronts are few. Plan the ride. Do not treat scarcity as an excuse for illicit markets.

Campus life generates folklore. Licensed retail generates receipts, labels, and ID checks. Choose the boring path.
