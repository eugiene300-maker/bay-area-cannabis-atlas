*A legal San Jose receipt does not rewrite HR policies, firearm forms, or federal banking friction.*

California adults can buy tested cannabis from SJPD-registered retailers and still collide with federal rules the moment money, employment, or firearms enter the chat. Adult-use legality under Proposition 64 is state law. Federal statutes and forms still shape ordinary South Bay life.

Banking remains awkward. Many banks and card networks treat marijuana-related businesses as high risk because adult-use cannabis remains federally prohibited for most commercial purposes. Shoppers feel that as cash-heavy counters, cashless ATM fees, and declined debit attempts. Medical Schedule III developments may eventually ease some operator-side tax and banking pathways for qualifying medical activity; they do not instantly turn every adult-use purchase into a normal credit-card grocery swipe. Bring cash backup.

Jobs are a second collision point. California has statutes limiting some employment discrimination related to off-duty cannabis use and limiting certain test interpretations, but exceptions exist—especially for safety-sensitive roles, federal contractors, and positions governed by federal drug-free workplace rules. Silicon Valley employers vary. A legal weekend purchase can still matter for roles that follow federal standards or company policies that remain restrictive. This article is not employment-law advice; it is a warning not to confuse a San Jose receipt with HR clearance. Read your handbook. Ask counsel or HR—not a budtender—about workplace policy.

Firearms forms are a third collision. Federal Form 4473 asks about unlawful controlled substance use. Cannabis remains a controlled substance under federal law for many purposes, and ATF materials have long warned that marijuana users are prohibited from possessing firearms under federal statute. State-legal medical or adult-use status does not automatically erase federal firearms prohibitions. People navigating both cannabis and firearm ownership need specialized legal counsel—not a newspaper explainer inventing workarounds.

Travel and immigration also sit outside local retail cheerfulness. Flying with cannabis through SJC remains a bad idea. Noncitizens should not treat California retail as immigration advice.

What shoppers can control: buy from registered retailers so product is tested and documented; keep consumption private and lease-compliant; separate cannabis spending from workplaces that forbid it; and do not invent federal loopholes from viral headlines. SJPD registration solves city legality. It does not rewrite the U.S. Code.

The mature local message is compartmentalization. Use the legal market for what it offers—testing, labels, accountability—without pretending a District 7 receipt federalizes your bank, your badge, or your Form 4473. Federal law still shapes local life. Shop accordingly.
