# Cannabis Journalism Ethics for a Local Shopper Paper

*Deal calendars and enforcement posts can live in one paper if the ethics are explicit: verify, disclose, refuse fake balance, and protect readers from harm.*

A San Jose cannabis shopper paper sits in a tricky genre. Readers want deals, product literacy, and neighborhood logistics. They also deserve journalism habits that do not collapse into advertorial fog or scare-mongering. Ethics are how we keep both promises.

Verification comes first. Storefront claims should be checked against SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses list. Event details should be checked on primary pages, then dated. Crime stories should stick to what police accounts actually said—without inventing cartels behind every bag. Health claims should defer to agencies like DCC and CDPH and to clinicians, not to anonymous forum certainty. When we refresh an enforcement lead, we scrub rumor and keep chronology clear.

Independence needs boundaries. This paper’s slate reserves a small number of explicitly labeled retailer feature slots at the end; the surrounding articles stay neutral and do not tour competing shops as featured destinations. Brand names may appear as shelf context. Paid deals should be distinguishable from reported facts. Readers should never have to guess whether a percentage came from a press release or from a checked event page.

Harm reduction outranks clickbait. Edible safety pieces include Poison Control numbers. Pregnancy coverage repeats state guidance to avoid cannabis and consult doctors rather than improvising counter advice. Youth access stays non-negotiable: we do not publish tips that help minors buy. Illicit-market “bargains” are framed as risks, not lifestyle hacks.

Context beats false equivalence. Licensed retail has taxes, testing, and paperwork; illicit retail may be cheaper and more dangerous. Explaining that difference is not “bias toward dispensaries.” It is consumer protection in a city that publishes a registration roster for a reason. Likewise, covering SJPD seizures does not mean every adult consumer is a suspect.

Language matters. We avoid ethnic stereotypes around cannabis crime. We avoid mocking faith communities. We avoid turning medical patients into props. We use “adults 21 and older” when discussing adult-use rules, and we keep medical lanes distinct when documentation changes limits.

Corrections are part of ethics. Menus change. Event tickets change. If a phone number or address shifts, updates should be explicit. Word-of-mouth exclusives that cannot be sourced stay out.

Transparency with readers builds trust over ninety articles: we say when something is evergreen Q&A, when something is tied to an August–September 2026 enforcement post, and when something is a featured retailer profile. We keep sources listed. We keep images real and credited—no generic stock pretending to be Commercial Street.

Local cannabis journalism will not look like a national politics desk. It can still be honest. Verify the roster. Quote the agency. Separate ads from advice. Refuse tips that get people arrested, poisoned, or scammed. That is the ethics floor under every shopping checklist in this paper.

Local shoppers can keep this guidance beside the other essentials of adult-use life in San Jose: verify sellers on SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses list, keep receipts, store products away from children and pets, and consume only on private property where household rules allow. Those habits will not settle every debate, but they keep ordinary weekends inside the lawful, lower-drama lane this market was built to support.
